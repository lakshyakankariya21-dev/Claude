Identity concepts · Round one

Ask Sale Wala

Five marks and a wordmark for a crockery, handloom and household essentials brand. The palette is vat indigo and brass — the dye of Indian handloom and the metal of the Indian kitchen — deliberately away from the terracotta-and-cream everyone else in home goods is already wearing. Every mark below is drawn as vector, works in one colour, and survives being shrunk to a 16px favicon.

01

The Stack

Plate, bowl, cup — reduced to three stacked rules inside a ring. It reads as tableware up close and as a tidy catalogue list at thumbnail size.

Where it winsStrongest at 16px. The ring gives it a built-in app-icon shape, so the favicon and the shop signboard are the same drawing.

02

Bazaar Arch

The arched shopfront every Indian high street shares, with a brass bowl set in the opening. It says 'this is a shop' before anyone reads a word.

Where it winsBest for signage, carry bags and the storefront. The open base lets it sit on top of a shelf edge or a photo without a box.

03

Warp & Weft

A literal plain weave: indigo warp threads running under and over a brass weft. The handloom half of the range, drawn as structure rather than as a picture of cloth.

Where it winsThe most ownable mark here — nobody else in home essentials is using a weave grid. Tiles seamlessly, so it doubles as packaging and label pattern.

04

Wala Seal

A plate seen from directly above is already concentric rings; so is a trust seal. This mark is both at once, which is why it carries the bilingual lockup.

Where it winsThe choice if you want to read as established rather than new. Stamps beautifully in one colour on cartons, tags and invoices.

05

Thread to Plate

One continuous line starts as a spun thread and closes as a plate rim — handloom and crockery in a single unbroken stroke. The riskiest mark and the most memorable.

Where it winsNeeds a minimum size of about 24px to keep the spiral open. Pair it with the wordmark; it is not a standalone signboard.

Bilingual lockup

Why it matters“Wala” is already doing the work of speaking Hindi in an English wordmark. Setting the name in Devanagari underneath doubles the audience on packaging, delivery boxes and local print — and it is what the market leaders do the moment they go past metro cities. Any of the five marks can take this treatment.

Wordmark, standalone

Where it winsInvoices, email signatures, marketplace seller banners and anywhere the mark would be too small to read. The woven rule under the name is lifted straight from concept 03, so the family stays tied together.

Using the marks

  • Clear space. Keep empty margin equal to the height of the letter A in the wordmark on all four sides.
  • Minimum size. Marks 01–04 hold at 16px. Mark 05 needs 24px.
  • One colour. Every mark is drawn to work as solid indigo on porcelain, or solid porcelain on indigo. Use that version for embossing, stamps and single-colour printing.
  • Never add a drop shadow, gradient, outline or stretch the lockup non-proportionally.
  • Photography. On product shots, place the porcelain-on-indigo version in a solid block rather than sitting the mark directly on the image.
  • Typeface. Archivo Black for the name, Archivo for supporting text, Tiro Devanagari Hindi for the Hindi setting. All three are free and open licensed.
