The ASK Monogram

A · S · K — Ashok, Sandhya, Kankariya — set as three initials and bound in a woven ring. A family name on a maker’s seal, not a shop sign.

00The story
ASK stands for Ashok, Sandhya and Kankariya — a family name, on everything that goes into your home. Crockery shaped from clay, textiles off the handloom, essentials chosen the way we’d choose them for our own house.

Everything below follows from that sentence. ASK is read as a monogram throughout — never as the English verb — and the three letters are always spaced so they register as initials rather than a word.

01The monogram

The initials are drawn, not typed: constant-width threads with an organic wobble, so the mark reads as made rather than set. The weave lives in the binding ring, where a cross-thread passes alternately over and under two running threads — a genuine plain weave.

The letters

The family

A, S and K, spaced so they read as three initials. Custom-drawn threads — no typeface was used, so the mark cannot be typed out by anyone else.

The binding

The handloom

Cross-threads passing over and under two running threads. Not a decorative border — it is drawn as the structure cloth is actually made of.

The seal

The maker

A closed round bound edge is the form of a stamp pressed into clay. It is what lets the same drawing sit on a pot base, a fabric label and an app icon.

Full colourIn a real weave the warp and weft are different threads. The full-colour cut says so: indigo running threads, turmeric cross-threads, terracotta initials.

02Lockups

A S K is the hero, set with wide tracking so the separation between letters is unmistakable. SALE WALA sits beneath, smaller and lighter. SALE is never coloured, never enlarged, never emphasised — emphasised, it reads as a clearance outlet and the positioning collapses.

04Colour

06Alternates

Concepts B–E from the brief, drawn and tested at full size, at 46 px and reversed. Concept B is where the primary came from; the other three are recorded here with the reason each was set aside.

Concept B

The Family Seal

Initials inside a circular maker’s stamp with a woven binding ring. This is the concept the primary mark grew out of — B and the primary converged.

Concept C

Monogram in a Vessel

The three letters nested in a thrown bowl. Signals crockery immediately, but the bowl crowds the letters and the K loses its arm below 40 px.

Concept D

Stacked Initials

A, S and K stacked as three bands between weft threads — folded textiles. Superb at small sizes, but a vertical stack reads as a list, not a name.

Concept E

The Ligature K

A and K share a diagonal; the S threads through the join. The most elegant, and the least legible — the shared stroke makes the A ambiguous.

07Constraints

Every hard constraint in the brief, and honestly how the mark meets it.

  • Works in one flat colourEvery cut is a single stroke colour. monogram-ceramic-stamp.svg thickens the strokes for debossing into clay; monogram-fabric-label.svg simplifies them for woven labels and embroidery.
  • Legible at 16 × 16 px, all three letters distinguishableOnly with the 16 px cut, which drops the binding ring and lets A, S and K fill the tile. The woven binding does not survive below about 44 px — that is a real limit of the mark, not something to design around.
  • Survives embossing / debossingNo hairlines. The lightest element in the primary is the binding thread at 10/300 of the mark; the stamp cut raises the letters to 30/300.
  • Works reversedSupplied on terracotta and on indigo, in undyed cotton.
  • The monogram alone carries recognitionThe three initials are the mark. No wordmark is required, and the seal shape is distinctive at a glance on a ceramic base or a bag.
  • Vector-clean geometryAll path data, no raster texture. Wordmark letterforms are outlined, so no font is needed to open or print the files.
08Do not
  • Never read ASK as the English verb — no question marks, speech bubbles, chat icons, magnifying glasses or FAQ imagery
  • Never set SALE in red, bold, or larger than the monogram
  • No shopping cart, price tag, percentage or starburst
  • No gradients, glows, drop shadows or 3D
  • No detailed pottery illustration
  • No compass-perfect circles — the wobble in the binding is deliberate
  • No stock bowls, generic leaves or swooshes
  • No invented or garbled Devanagari
